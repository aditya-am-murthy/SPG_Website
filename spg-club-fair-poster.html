--- ## Who We Are The Science Policy Group (SPG) is a student-led, non-partisan organization at UCLA dedicated to bridging the gap between scientific research and public policy. Founded in 2018, we connect scientists, engineers, and policy enthusiasts from across the university to advocate for evidence-based decision-making. We believe scientists have a responsibility — and a right — to engage with the policy decisions that shape our world. SPG provides the training, community, and opportunities to do exactly that. --- ## Notable Activities - **#MakingOurCase 2025** — Members traveled to Washington, D.C. for a prestigious science advocacy workshop and met with Congressional offices (April 2025) - **Stand Up For Science March** — SPG joined thousands of scientists and allies rallying for evidence-based policy in downtown Los Angeles (March 2025) - **AAAS Annual Meeting** — Members attended the nation's largest general scientific society conference in Washington, D.C. (February 2025) - **CCST Science & Technology Policy Fellowship** — Joan Chimezie selected as a California Council on Science and Technology Science Translation Fellow (February 2025) - **Science Advocacy Workshop** — Hands-on training in translating research into actionable policy recommendations (June 2025) - **Community Voices in AI** — A community forum bridging AI research and public understanding of its policy implications (May 2024) - **Clean Transportation Panel** — Co-hosted with E3, featuring speakers from industry and government on clean transportation policy (April 2024) - **Science Policy Career Night** — Students connected with professionals from the NIH, EPA, RAND Corporation, and a Congressional office (December 2023) --- ## Instagram **@scipolucla** *(verify handle before printing)* --- ## What Involvement Looks Like Membership is open to all UCLA students — undergraduate and graduate — regardless of major or prior experience in policy. **Science Advocacy** — Learn to communicate your research to policymakers, write op-eds and policy briefs, and represent the scientific community in legislative settings. **Outreach & Education** — Plan and run science-themed events for the broader Los Angeles community, including K–12 students and the general public. **Science Communication** — Develop skills to translate complex research into accessible language for non-expert audiences through writing, media, and public speaking. **External Relations** — Build and maintain partnerships with government agencies, think tanks, nonprofits, and scientific societies. Help coordinate visits and collaborations. **Career Development** — Attend career panels, connect with mentors in science policy, and learn about pathways into government, NGOs, and policy research organizations. **Policy Research** — Work on in-depth research projects on topics like AI regulation, climate legislation, public health infrastructure, and emerging technology policy. Members typically attend biweekly general meetings and join one or two working groups based on their interests. There is no application — just show up.
